What changes when access changes?

Track meaningful changes in market structure—direct access, new settlement systems, and parallel networks—without confusing policy, permission and live operation.

An infrastructure change can alter who is eligible to connect, who carries liquidity, who settles and what a market can offer. A proposed rule is not an operating rail.
01 · CANADA · CAD
PLANNED

RTR changes the available clearing and settlement architecture

Payments Canada targets a sequenced Q4 2026 launch; as of the review date, its own site describes RTR as not yet launched. The Bank of Canada has a published settlement-account eligibility policy for the planned system.

What has not changed: Eligibility policy does not prove an institution has a live account or has started settling RTR payments.

02 · UK · GBP
LIVE POLICY

Eligible non-banks can settle directly in Bank of England RTGS

FCA-authorised payment and e-money institutions have been eligible for settlement accounts since 2017, subject to the Bank’s criteria and onboarding.

What has not changed: An RTGS settlement account is not a banking licence, not a reserve account, and not proof of universal direct participation in every scheme.

03 · BRAZIL · BRL
LIVE

Pix offers direct and indirect access to central-bank settlement

The central bank operates Pix’s SPI. Eligible direct participants hold dedicated instant-payment accounts at BCB; indirect participants use a direct participant or special settlement agent.

What has not changed: Offering Pix at the customer interface is not the same as directly settling through SPI.

04 · UNITED STATES · USD
LIVE

Instant payments now run on parallel infrastructures

FedNow and RTP are distinct systems operated by the Federal Reserve Banks and The Clearing House respectively. They are not one interchangeable rail.

What has not changed: One network’s participant list or technical certification cannot establish another network’s access or bilateral interoperability.
